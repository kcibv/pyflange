    PyFlange¶

    Open-source Python library for the fatigue analysis of large bolted ring-flange connections, implementing the polynomial flange segment model of IEC 61400-6 AMD1.

    PyFlange computes the bolt axial force and bolt bending moment of a flange segment as a function of the tower shell force, including the effect of flange gaps (flatness imperfections) and flange inclination. On top of this model it provides the bolt fatigue assessment of IEC 61400-6 and the tools for probabilistic (Monte Carlo) analyses.

    It is meant for engineers who design or check the L-flanges and T-flanges of wind turbine towers, monopiles and transition pieces, and who need a fast, transparent and verifiable implementation of the standard.

    from pyflange.flangesegments import PolynomialLFlangeSegment

fseg = PolynomialLFlangeSegment(a=0.150, b=0.122, s=0.054, t=0.172, R=4.0, ...)
Fs = fseg.bolt_axial_force(500e3)       # bolt force for a 500 kN shell force

    Background¶

    The Bolt & Beautiful project¶

    PyFlange has been developed within Bolt & Beautiful, a GROW research project on the bolted connections of offshore wind turbine support structures, by KCI, Siemens Gamesa, JBO and TNO. The project is funded with subsidy from the Top Sector Energy of the Dutch Ministry of Economic Affairs.

    Within the project, extensive finite element studies were carried out. They supported the calibration of the safety factors and small improvements of the analytical flange model, which are proposed for the next edition of IEC 61400-6 (ref. [5], section 1.1).

    The link with IEC 61400-6¶

    IEC 61400-6 is the international standard for the design of wind turbine towers and foundations. Its amendment 1 (AMD1, published in June 2025) replaced the former Schmidt/Neuper approach for the fatigue design of flange bolts with a new analytical model, developed by Marc Seidel (Siemens Gamesa), which covers the effect of flange gaps of the size allowed by the manufacturing tolerances (ref. [1]). The model is given in Annex G.2 of the standard (ref. [2]), and the use of AMD1 becomes mandatory for IEC certification from December 2026 (ref. [5], section 9.2).

    The model is considerably more complex than the previous one. To help the industry implement it, PyFlange was developed as an open-source reference implementation, and it is referred to in the background document of the standard (ref. [5], section 9.2). The results of PyFlange are verified against the reference spreadsheets of Siemens Gamesa (see Validation).

    The revision of the standard towards Edition 2 (ED2) introduces some small changes to the model and new design gap heights (ref. [5], section 9.7). PyFlange implements them in separate classes (PolynomialLFlangeSegmentED2, PolynomialTFlangeSegmentED2, gap_height_distribution_ED2), so that both versions can be compared.

    ED2 is a draft

    The ED2 implementation follows the draft background document of 2026-08-04 (ref. [5]). It may change before ED2 is published. Use the AMD1 classes for certified designs.

    The documents cited as ref. [n] throughout this documentation are listed on the References page.
